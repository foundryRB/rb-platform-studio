**Date**: 2026-07-04
**Status**: Decided

> **Amended by the platform's ADR-036.** The onboarding PR is merged automatically by the platform repo's CI moments after it opens; the per-PR human merge is retired, and the authority moment is invoking the onboarding (naming the app and granting its hostname). PR-mode itself stands: the studio still never commits to `main`, every change still rides an `onboard/<app>` PR gated by the conformance suite, and the evidence trail below is unchanged. Only the merge actor moved from a maintainer to CI.

## Context

`platform/` in the platform repo is the cluster control plane: ArgoCD reconciles `main` directly into the cluster. Anything that lands on `main` is live infrastructure. The platform's onboarding process has always had a maintainer gate: admitting an app and granting its hostname are deliberate decisions; the rest is mechanical.

## Decision

The studio never commits to `main`. Every onboarding action produces exactly one pull request:

- **Branch**: `onboard/<app>` for app onboardings, `onboard/<app>-db` for database grants.
- **Title**: the conventional commit message of the change.
- **Body**: a summary, the changed files, a baseline-vs-branch conformance table, and a maintainer checklist.

The human merge IS the maintainer gate. This is the one structural difference from the playbook-studio chassis, whose datastore is not a control plane and which commits to `main` directly.

## Rationale

- A merge to the platform's `main` is a deployment; an automated surface writing it directly would collapse the deliberate part of onboarding (admission, hostname grant) into the mechanical part.
- One PR per onboarding action keeps the maintainer's decision the smallest reviewable unit, and the conformance table in the body puts the gate's evidence where the decision is made.
- Reusing the platform's existing gate rather than inventing a studio-side approval flow keeps one authority moment, in the place it already lives.

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Commit to `main` directly, as the chassis does**: correct where the datastore is plain content; wrong where `main` is reconciled infrastructure, because every accepted change would go live without the admission decision.
- **A studio-internal approval queue with its own reviewers model**: rebuilds GitHub's review surface behind a second credential and detaches the decision from the audit trail the platform already has.
